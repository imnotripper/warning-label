# Candidate selection — Warning Label for Humans

Competition: `u-0xb3e689-warning-label-for-humans-one-line-big-laugh-40e31f85`
Brief: funniest single-line safety warning label that should come printed on every human being,
max 160 characters, funny first, plausible second.
Judge criteria (in order): (1) on-subject gate — reads as a warning label for humans; (2) the laugh,
turn arriving in the last few words; (3) recognition — instantly relatable human flaw; (4) label
wording — reads like a real printed warning ("WARNING:", "CAUTION:", "Do not...", hazard-style
phrasing); (5) fits inside 160 chars, no filler, no second idea.

Field study: not possible — blind rounds with no visible leaderboard, so the build went straight
to the multi-candidate duel below. Duel method: the winner was compared pairwise against every
other candidate in turn, criteria applied in order, first difference deciding unless a later
criterion differed by much more (per the judge brief).

## The 10 candidates (all ≤160 chars, character counts verified with Node `[...s].length`)

1. [102] `WARNING: This human will say 'I'm five minutes away' while still in bed. Do not plan around this unit.`
2. [100] `CAUTION: Contents may suddenly remember an embarrassing moment from 2009 and go silent mid-sentence.`

8. [98] `CAUTION: Human overheats when told 'calm down.' Keep away from open flames and unsolicited advice.`
9. [98] `WARNING: This human replays arguments in the shower and wins them all. Do not engage after 11 p.m.`
10. [98] `CAUTION: May say 'one more episode' at 9 p.m. and surface at 3 a.m. questioning every life choice.`

## Pairwise duel (criteria in order, first difference decides)

- #7 vs #9 (final): both pass (1) and (4) cleanly. On (2) #7's turn lands in the last few words —
  "defended for 30 years" — after the setup "formed in 30 seconds", a neat 30-seconds/30-years
  contrast; #9's "wins them all" is the expected punch of a well-worn shower-argument trope. On (3)
  #7 is more universal: everyone knows someone who formed an opinion instantly and defends it
  forever, while #9 needs the shower-arguing habit. #7 wins.
- #7 vs #5: #5 has a good double beat (wrong directions, then blames the map), but its turn is
  smeared across the middle, not the last few words; #7's punch is the final phrase. #7 wins on (2).
- #7 vs #8: #8's "overheats when told 'calm down'" is a strong label image, but the second sentence
  ("Keep away from open flames and unsolicited advice") splits the joke into two ideas; #7 is one
  idea, one punch. #7 wins on (5).
- #7 vs #1: #1 is relatable but its punch ("Do not plan around this unit") is an instruction, not a
  laugh; the lie itself is the whole joke and it lands early. #7 wins on (2).
- #7 vs #10: #10's "questioning every life choice" is a good ending, but "one more episode" is a
  familiar binge trope and the line leans on a specific habit (streaming); #7's flaw is broader.
  #7 wins on (3).
- #7 vs #2/#3/#4/#6: all pass (1) and (4); none matches #7 on (2) — #2's punch is buried mid-line,
  #3's "nowhere to be" is a mild closer, #4's blame-the-battery joke resolves before the end, #6's
  "blame the fridge" repeats its own setup. #7 wins each pairing.
- #9 is the runner-up: the shower-argument replay is instantly recognised and "Do not engage after
  11 p.m." is proper label wording; it loses the final only because its punch is the expected one
  for its trope, while #7's 30-seconds/30-years contrast is fresher and lands last.

## Winner

**#7 — `WARNING: Do not shake. Contents contain opinions formed in 30 seconds and defended for 30 years.` (96 chars)**

Rationale: best on criteria (2) and (3) — the sharpest turn in the set, arriving in the final
phrase ("defended for 30 years") against the "formed in 30 seconds" setup, and the most universal
human flaw (instant opinion, lifelong defence). Opens with a genuine hazard header and the classic
label instruction "Do not shake", reads exactly like a printed contents warning, one idea, no
filler, 96 of 160 characters used.
